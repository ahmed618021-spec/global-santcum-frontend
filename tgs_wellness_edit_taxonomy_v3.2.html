Article Tagging System
How Tags Work

Tags are free-text keywords added to each article in Sanity. They are separate from the category — the category is the primary classification, tags are additional descriptors that aid discovery, SEO, and related article suggestions.

Tags serve three purposes: (1) Internal filtering — allows readers to find related articles across categories. (2) SEO — tags matching common search terms help Google surface your articles. (3) Related content — the platform uses shared tags to surface "You might also enjoy" article suggestions.

Tagging rules: Always lowercase. 6–10 tags per article. Specific enough to be meaningful, broad enough to connect to future articles. Never duplicate the category name as a tag.

SEO Strategy
Overview

SEO for The Wellness Edit operates on two levels: the blog index page (/the-wellness-edit) and each individual article page. Both require distinct SEO treatment. All SEO fields are managed in Sanity Studio — no developer work required once the schema is built.

The Wellness Edit is TGS's primary SEO asset. Well-optimised articles build organic authority over time, drive discovery from people searching for wellness experiences, and bring retreat hosts and wellness guests into the platform without paid acquisition.

The blog index page has fixed SEO metadata that does not change article to article. This is set once by JR in the Next.js page file.

Individual Article Pages — SEO Title Rules

The SEO title is what appears as the blue link text in Google search results. It is separate from the article headline — it can and should be written differently. Fill in the seoTitle field in Sanity for every article. If left empty, the article title is used as fallback.

RuleDetail
Maximum length60 characters including the brand suffix. Google truncates anything longer.
Format[Article topic or keyword phrase] | The Wellness Edit. Example: "How to Choose Your First Retreat | The Wellness Edit"
Lead with the keywordPut the most searchable phrase first. Google weights the beginning of the title more heavily.
Different from the headlineThe article headline is for readers. The SEO title is for Google. They should complement each other but don't need to match. A headline like "The Room Speaks First" is evocative but not searchable — the SEO title should be "Why Retreat Space Design Matters | The Wellness Edit"
No clickbaitThe title must accurately represent the article content. Misleading titles hurt Google ranking.
Individual Article Pages — SEO Description Rules

The meta description appears as the grey paragraph text beneath the title in Google search results. It does not directly affect ranking but dramatically affects whether someone clicks. Fill in the seoDescription field in Sanity for every article. If left empty, the excerpt is used as fallback — so always write a strong excerpt too.

RuleDetail
Maximum length155–160 characters. Google truncates longer descriptions with an ellipsis.
Include a benefit or hookTell the reader what they will learn or gain. "Discover why the space matters as much as the facilitator — and what to look for when choosing a retreat venue."
Include a natural keywordWork in one or two search terms naturally. If someone searches "how to choose a retreat venue" your description should contain those words.
Write in active voiceActive voice is more compelling. "Discover, learn, find out" rather than "This article covers, this piece discusses".
Do not repeat the titleThe description should add new information, not restate the title.

Image Alt Text Rules

Every image uploaded to Sanity requires alt text. This applies to the hero image and any inline images within the article body.

RuleDetail
Be descriptive and specificDescribe exactly what is in the image. "A single yoga mat on a sunlit studio floor, geometric window shadows cast across the space" — not "yoga studio".
Maximum length125 characters. Screen readers read the full alt text aloud — keep it concise.
Do not start with "image of" or "photo of"Screen readers already announce it as an image. Start directly with the description.
Include context where relevantIf the image relates to a specific location, modality, or person — include it. "Traditional Japanese onsen pool surrounded by cedar forest, Nagano Prefecture".
SEO benefitGoogle Images indexes alt text. Descriptive, keyword-relevant alt text helps articles rank in image search results — a secondary but valuable traffic source.
